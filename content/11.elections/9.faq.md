::accordion{multiple}
  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Do we really need to do this?

  #default
  Yes.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  We need to make somebody a committer right now. How do we do this?

  #default
  You don’t.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Why is it taking a long time to grant a new committer the necessary privileges?

  #default
  The first thing that you need to do is check that the election has run its course: an election will run for a full week before it is complete. Has a full week passed since the election was started?

  A successful election requires approval from the PMC. The PMC will be notified automatically via their mailing list when their approval is required. If the PMC has not yet approved the election, connect directly with them for help.

  Following a successful election and PMC approval, the provisioning process will be automatically initiated. A new committer will receive an automated email request with instructions regarding next steps (which may include signing a committer agreement). Ask the new committer to check their spam folder.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Can I still be a committer if I change employers?

  #default
  Yes. Your status as a committer is not tied to your employment status. You may, however, require [paperwork](/committer-paperwork/) from your new employer (or if you become self-employed). If you change employers, please contact the [EMO Records Team](mailto:emo-records@eclipse-foundation.org) for help regarding paperwork requirements.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  What happens if committers don’t vote?

  #default
  If a project has three or fewer committers, then all committers must vote. If even one out of the three does not vote, then the election will end in failure. If the non-voting committer is also not active, then they can, perhaps, be [retired](/elections/committer-retirement/) by the project lead.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Can we change a merit statement?

  #default
  The election infrastructure doesn’t permit changing a merit statement after an election has begun (the principle being that changing the question after you start a vote is considered bad form).

  In cases where the merit statement is omitted or considered inadequate, or committers have additional questions about a candidate, it’s completely reasonable to discuss it on the mailing list, and for committers to then use that discussion as the basis for their vote. Committers who have already voted, can return to the election record and change their vote.

  Committers can also add information via the comment that accompanies their vote on an election.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Can a committer change their vote on an election?

  #default
  Yes. A committer can change their vote while an election is still in progress.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  How do we transfer committers from one project to another?

  #default
  Short answer: you don’t.

  We have no concept of transferring committers. if committers need to move from one project to another, then they must be elected as committers to the new project and retire themselves from the old one.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Can a project lead retire a *disruptive* committer?

  #default
  The EDP allows a project lead to retire a disruptive committer. This is basically the "nuclear" option and so should be used sparingly; we have only retired a few committers in this manner. Should a project lead decide to take this step, they should be prepared to provide evidence to back up the claim of disruption. The project lead should work with the PMC and EMO to determine how (or if) that evidence is tracked and disseminated.
  :::
