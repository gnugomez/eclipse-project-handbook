::accordion{multiple}
  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Can a progress review fail?

  #default
  Technically, yes. A release review can fail. In our history, however, this occurs very rarely. We set up release reviews to succeed.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Do we really need to do this?

  #default
  Yes.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  How often should we do releases?

  #default
  This depends very much on the nature of your project and the expectations of your community and stake holders. If you’re not sure, connect with your [PMC](/project-roles/project-management-committee/) for guidance.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  How much effort should we put into this?

  #default
  The amount of effort varies based on the nature of the team, and expectations of the community and stake holders. Generally, though, a project team shouldn’t spend more than a couple of hours working directly on the formal aspects of a progress review. If the amount of effort seems too onerous, you may be trying too hard. Connect with your [PMC](/project-roles/project-management-committee/) or the [EMO](mailto:emo@eclipse-foundation.org) for guidance.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Do I need to engage in a progress review?

  #default
  If the project team wants to issue an official major or minor release and has not engaged in a progress review within a year of the planned release date, then a progress review is required.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  What is the difference between a release review and progress review?

  #default
  In practice, there is really no difference. The activities involved are the same and a project may create major and minor releases for an entire year following a successful release or progress review.

  Progress reviews were added to the [Eclipse Foundation Development Process](/development-process/) primarily to support the [Eclipse Foundation Specification Process](https://www.eclipse.org/projects/efsp/), which requires that specification project teams engage periodic reviews that do not necessarily align with releases.

  In cases where, for example, a project team has gone an extended period of time without having engaged in a review, the project leadership chain may compel the project team to engage in a progress review to ensure that the project team is following the processes and is generally engaging in the sorts of activities required for success.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  How do I obtain PMC approval?

  #default
  Send the the [Project Management Committee](/project-roles/project-management-committee/) (PMC) a note via the top-level project’s *PMC* mailing list with a link to the release record. Note that the release record page has a handy link labelled **Committer Tools** › **Send Email the PMC**.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  I need to do a release now. Can you fast-track the review?

  #default
  While we do try to be as accommodating as possible, the answer is no. We have a well-defined process with predictable dates. Please plan accordingly.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Can a project in the incubation phase do releases?

  #default
  Yes. In fact, we encourage projects to do at least one release while in [incubation phase](/incubation/).
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  What restrictions are placed on version names for incubating projects?

  #default
  Projects in the [incubation phase](/incubation/) generally use version numbers that are less than 1.0. This is, however, a convention not a rule. If it makes sense for you community and adopters to use higher numbers, then do so. If you’re not sure, ask your [Project Management Committee](/project-roles/project-management-committee/) (PMC) for advice.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  How do I name/number milestone builds?

  #default
  Milestone builds must be named in a manner that that conveys the *pre-release* nature of the content. It is common for project teams to name their milestone builds *alpha* or *beta*. Another convention is to use the name/number of the eventual release suffixed with "Mn" (e.g. the second milestone for Eclipse EGit 3.2 may have a file named `egit-3.2M2`). Projects in the [incubation phase](/incubation/) may produce milestone builds for their graduation release, e.g `myProject-1.0M2`.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Can Eclipse open source projects in the incubation phase distribute milestone builds?

  #default
  Yes. In fact, the EMO strongly encourages Eclipse open source projects in the [incubation phase](/incubation/) to create and distribute milestone builds.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  How can I get help?

  #default
  Post your question on the [incubation mailing list](/incubation/incubation-mailing-list/), or contact your [Project Management Committee](/project-roles/project-management-committee/) (PMC) or the [EMO](mailto:emo@eclipse-foundation.org).
  :::
