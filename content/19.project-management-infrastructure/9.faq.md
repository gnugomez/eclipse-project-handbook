  #title
  Why doesn’t a committer appear on the project’s *Who’s Involved* page?

  #default
  For a committer to appear on a project’s *Who’s Involved* page:

  1.  The new committer must be [elected](/elections/committer-elections/) (or be listed as an initial committer on a [project proposal](/starting-a-project/project-proposal/));

  2.  The election must complete successfully and be approved by the PMC;

  3.  The new committer must provide the required [paperwork](/committer-paperwork/);

  4.  The paperwork must be processed by the EMO Records Team; and

  5.  The Eclipse Infrastructure Team must create the committer account.

  Each piece takes time. If a committer doesn’t appear some number of days after the successful completion of a committer election, then contact the committer to confirm that they’ve followed the provided instructions and have submitted committer paperwork. Then, connect with [EMO](mailto:emo@eclipse-foundation.org) for assistance.

  If the new committer will be working with projects that use GitHub, they must be sure to add their GitHub Id to their [Eclipse Foundation Account](/contributing/eclipse-foundation-account/) so that our scripts can add them to the GitHub Teams that we use to manage access to Eclipse Foundation repositories hosted on GitHub.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  Why doesn’t a particular company’s logo appear as an active member company?

  #default
  The *Overview* and *Who’s Involved* pages in the PMI display [company logos](/project-management-infrastructure/editing-project-metadata/company-logos/) in the *Active Member Companies* section.

  Company logos are displayed automatically based on the contribution record. For a company logo to appear:

  1.  The company must be a member of the Eclipse Foundation;

  2.  The company must have provided the Eclipse Foundation with their logo via the [member company portal](https://membership.eclipse.org/portal); and

  3.  At least one developer who works for that company must have authored a commit within the last three months.

  For this to work, the developer must use the email address that they’ve used to register their [Eclipse Foundation account](/contributing/eclipse-foundation-account/) on the commit, and they must [identify their employer](/contributing/eclipse-foundation-account/#employed-by-a-member-company) in their Eclipse Foundation Account.

  > **Tip:** If you are aware of any member company that meets this criteria and are not listed, please contact [EMO](mailto:emo@eclipse-foundation.org). The problem is usually that the contributor is not correctly aligned with their organisation.
  :::

  :::accordion-item
  #title
  What is the difference between a *Committer* and an *Historic Committer*?

  #default
  The *Who’s Involved* page lists committers and (in many cases) historic committers. Committers are those individuals who currently hold committer status (that is, they can write to project resources). Historic committers are retired former committers. While they hold a special place in our hearts, they have no official status on the project. To turn an historic committer back into a committer, they must be [re-elected](/elections/committer-elections/) to the project.
  :::
